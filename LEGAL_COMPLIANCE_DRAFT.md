# ByeByeDoody — legal and operating checklist (draft for attorney review)

**Not legal advice. Not ready for signature or publication.** This draft is a planning checklist, not a guarantee of compliance. A California employment attorney and the City of Sacramento should review the actual business model, contracts, routes, waste handling, and payment flow before the platform hires providers or accepts commercial jobs.

## 1. Worker model must be selected first

### Option A — W-2 employees
Use this when ByeByeDoody controls the work, sets the schedule or rates, requires branded service, supervises performance, dispatches mandatory jobs, or treats drivers as part of its own cleanup operation.

Before the first shift, plan for:
- Employer registration and payroll setup with California EDD; federal EIN and payroll tax setup as applicable.
- Workers' compensation coverage before work begins.
- Written wage notices and accurate timekeeping, including required paid time, overtime, meal/rest periods, and final-pay rules.
- Reimbursement of necessary business expenses, including required vehicle/mileage expenses under applicable law.
- Required new-hire reporting, I-9 employment eligibility verification, tax withholding, payroll records, and required workplace notices.
- Required safety training, injury/illness prevention program, incident reporting, and any applicable heat/smoke/chemical safety procedures.
- A written commission/incentive plan reviewed for California Labor Code requirements. Incentives cannot replace minimum wage, overtime, expense reimbursement, or other statutory rights.

California's general statewide minimum wage is $16.90/hour effective January 1, 2026, unless a higher local or industry rate applies. Re-check the applicable rate when hiring.

### Option B — independent service providers referred through a genuine referral agency
California Labor Code section 2777 includes yard cleanup and animal services among services that may be referred through a referral agency. The exception is conditional; it does not apply merely because an agreement says “independent contractor.”

Counsel must confirm every condition is met in practice, including:
- Provider is a sole proprietor or qualifying business entity.
- Provider is free from the agency's control and direction.
- Provider certifies required local business license/tax registration and any applicable professional permit/licensure; retain required certifications for at least three years.
- Provider serves the customer under the provider's own business name.
- Provider supplies their own tools and supplies.
- Provider has an independently established business in the same or related trade.
- Provider can maintain other clients and work with competing referral agencies.
- Provider sets their own hours and terms or negotiates them directly with the client.
- Provider sets/negotiates their own rate or freely accepts/rejects the client's rate.
- Provider may accept or reject clients/contracts without penalty.
- The agency's services are limited to referrals and ancillary administrative services.
- The referral-agency fee is charged to the client and is not deducted from the provider's rate.

A platform that dictates provider prices, controls schedules, requires acceptance, penalizes declines, supervises the manner of cleanup, or presents providers as its employees may fail the exception. GPS must not be used as punitive or continuous worker surveillance. Do not use this model until counsel reviews the app design and real operating practices.

## 2. Sacramento waste collection and disposal — written determination required

Sacramento City Code Chapter 13.24 regulates collection, removal, transportation, and brokering of commercial waste, including putrescible organic material. The code states that commercial-waste collection/transport generally requires a valid City franchise, and brokering is lawful only when the service is with a franchised hauler. It also restricts commercial and multifamily generators from selecting an unfranchised collector, subject to statutory exceptions.

Before advertising “removal” or hauling material away, ask the City's Solid Waste Manager in writing:
1. Does pet feces collected from a single-family residential yard count as residential waste or commercial waste when removed by a paid dog-waste service?
2. Does the answer change for commercial properties, apartment/multifamily properties, HOA common areas, dog parks, or business campuses?
3. May ByeByeDoody or its referred providers place bagged pet waste in the customer's own approved refuse container?
4. May a provider transport bagged pet waste in a personal vehicle to a disposal facility? If so, which facilities accept it and under what conditions?
5. Does brokering/referring this service require ByeByeDoody to hold a franchise or use a franchised hauler?
6. What permits, franchise, vehicle, diversion-plan, or disposal records are required?

Until the City answers, do not enable jobs that require providers to haul waste off-site or promise commercial waste removal. A customer placing waste in their own authorized refuse container may be a different arrangement, but obtain confirmation.

## 3. Provider application and screening checklist

Collect only what is necessary, explain why, restrict access, and set deletion dates.

Suggested steps (subject to counsel and insurer approval):
- Legal name and contact information; verified email and phone.
- Age and eligibility to perform the role; do not collect extra identity data without a defined lawful need.
- Driver's license and driving record only if driving is an essential job function and the review is legally permissible.
- Vehicle registration and proof of required auto insurance if the provider uses a vehicle for work.
- Business license/tax registration certification if using the referral-agency model and required locally.
- Proof of general liability or other coverage only if required by the approved provider model/contract.
- Relevant experience and references, with consistent nondiscriminatory criteria.
- Safety orientation: dogs and animal behavior, gates/escape prevention, customer property, sanitation, PPE, sharps/unknown waste, heat exposure, vehicle loading, spills, incident escalation, and prohibited tasks.
- Written acknowledgment of privacy/location practices and emergency procedures.
- Background checks only where legally permitted, with required notices, authorization, pre-adverse/adverse action procedures, and individualized review where required. Never use an informal database check as a substitute for lawful screening.

Do not collect Social Security numbers, identity-document images, bank credentials, or full payment-card details through ordinary website forms or email. Use authorized payroll, identity-verification, and payment providers.

## 4. Customer terms — clauses counsel should draft

- Service description and clear distinction between yard cleanup, bagging, container placement, and off-site hauling.
- Exact property, access instructions, dog/animal safety responsibilities, and customer duty to secure animals.
- Service scope, quote, frequency, weather/access rescheduling, and what is excluded (for example, hazardous materials, sharps, aggressive animals, unsafe access, or unknown substances).
- Itemized provider service rate and separately disclosed platform/referral fee, if that model is approved.
- Payment authorization, taxes, receipts, cancellations, no-show, refunds, chargebacks, and complaint deadlines.
- Property damage and incident reporting; no blanket waiver of non-waivable consumer rights.
- Privacy, GPS/location sharing, photos, retention, customer support, and contact preferences.
- Emergency contact and safe access procedure.
- No guarantee of pest, odor, pathogen, or disease elimination unless specifically substantiated.

## 5. Provider agreement — only after classification approval

Agreement should match the real working relationship and cover:
- Correct legal classification and scope of relationship.
- Who contracts with the customer and who is responsible for the service.
- Rates, referral fee, payment timing, refunds, chargebacks, taxes, and records.
- Independent choice of schedule, clients, rates, and acceptance/rejection if using referral agency model.
- Required business registration, tools, supplies, vehicle and insurance responsibilities.
- Service quality and customer property rules that do not become impermissible control.
- Animal safety, sanitation, PPE, prohibited work, incident reporting, and emergency escalation.
- Privacy, location permission, data use, photos, confidentiality, and account security.
- Complaints, disputes, suspension, termination, and appeal process.
- Indemnity and insurance provisions drafted by counsel; avoid overbroad or unlawful waivers.
- No transfer of non-waivable statutory rights.

## 6. Privacy and GPS notice

Before collecting location:
- Explain which location is collected (device GPS, job address, or both), when, why, who can see it, and how long it is kept.
- Request explicit device permission; offer manual address/ZIP entry where possible.
- Default to no background tracking. If location is needed for a job, limit collection to the active job and only what is necessary.
- Share provider ETA/location with the assigned customer only for the active booking.
- Keep customer property coordinates and access notes private and role-restricted.
- Encrypt data in transit and at rest; log administrator access; delete raw location traces on a short documented schedule unless needed for a dispute or legal obligation.
- Provide access/correction/deletion channels and explain any legal retention exceptions.
- Review California privacy-law applicability. CCPA/CPRA thresholds determine whether the statute applies to a particular business, but precise geolocation is sensitive personal information for covered businesses. Other privacy, security, breach-notification, and employment rules may still apply.

## 7. Safety and incident procedures

- Do not enter a property if a dog is loose, aggressive, or not safely secured.
- Do not handle needles, sharps, chemicals, medical waste, dead animals, or unidentified hazardous material.
- Stop work and notify the customer/admin if access is unsafe, a gate is broken, a pet escapes, or property is damaged.
- Use gloves, hand hygiene, dedicated tools, secure bagging, vehicle containment, and approved disposal method.
- Document incidents without photographing people or unnecessary private areas.
- Establish emergency response, injury reporting, vehicle accident, bite, and exposure procedures.

## 8. Insurance and financial controls

Obtain broker advice for:
- General liability and care/custody/control exclusions.
- Commercial auto or business-use endorsements for every vehicle used for work.
- Workers' compensation and employer liability if employees are used.
- Cyber/privacy coverage if storing customer addresses, GPS, identity, or job photos.
- Provider coverage requirements if the referral model is approved.
- Clear rules for who bears customer refunds, chargebacks, property damage, and unrecovered payment balances.

## 9. Documents to have reviewed before launch

1. Customer Terms of Service and service authorization.
2. Privacy Policy and Notice at Collection.
3. GPS/Location Permission and Provider Tracking Notice.
4. W-2 Employee Offer Letter, Job Description, Wage/Commission Plan, and handbook OR Referral Agency Agreement plus Provider Terms (not both by default).
5. Customer cancellation/refund/dispute policy.
6. Safety, animal handling, sanitation, vehicle, waste disposal, and incident policies.
7. Insurance certificates and required local business/franchise/disposal authorizations.
8. Data-processing agreements with hosting, identity, messaging, maps, AI, and payment vendors.
9. Accessibility and nondiscrimination review.
10. Tax/payroll/accounting and Stripe Connect payment-flow review.
